What this exploration is claiming

Axis
The portrait already has its own routes, its own cache-buster and its own upload control. Either it earns the tile or the feature is over-built for 64 pixels.
Signature
The monogram. A character with no picture gets their initial cut into the page's own woven stripe in MedievalSharp — a mark, not a gap. Six portrait-less characters read as six monograms rather than six unfinished tiles, which is the failure mode of every portrait-led list.
Delete
A quiet text control in the label's foot; pressing it turns the label strip into the question and its two answers. The plate is never covered, so you can still see whose face you are about to delete. No dialog to dismiss.
Inversion
The whole plate opens the sheet, via a stretched ::after on the name control; the portrait upload moves to a corner pill. Today the largest control uploads a picture and the smallest opens the character. Worth arguing about, not worth leaving as it is.
Costs
Three dictionary keys (the confirm question and its two answers) in en.ts and fr.ts; a stretched-link pattern that must keep the portrait pill above it and must be disabled while the confirm strip is open; one new glyph for the camera pill — lucide:camera costs nothing in attribution, and only a game-icons glyph would mean an ICONS.txt edit. No API change, no /api/derive call.
Risks
A 4:5 plate is tall — six characters is already a scroll on a laptop, where the register in E fits fifteen. And the monogram only works while names are distinct; a party of Thalins is six identical plates.
Kept
Name and class line remain one control carrying characters.open; the portrait control keeps characters.portraitAdd / portraitChange as the whole of its accessible name; --card stays opaque and the stripe appears only where it is the subject.
