Exploration D — Arcane Schematic. The aesthetic counterproposal: not a surface pretending to be paper, but a working drawing of a person. The signature is that a derivation is drawn rather than described — components sit on stub lines that converge on a bus and terminate in the answer, so the total is visibly the end of a line instead of one more figure in a row of figures. Numbers are monospace, which is tabular by construction. The ground is two gradients, so it costs no image, no fetch and no fourth licence, and it flattens to plain colour in the PDF without the export drifting from the screen.

What it costs. A palette swap in styles.css and the same swap again in pdf/theme.ts, plus a Derivation component for the drawn diagrams — which is presentation over fields DerivedSheet already carries, so no arithmetic moves to the client. The PDF wants a light-on-print variant of this palette rather than the screen one; a dark export is a cartridge problem, not a design one. The real question it asks: the leather is the most expensive thing on the page and nothing is read on it — is it worth the contrast it costs everything above it? This direction says no. What it risks: reading as an engineering tool rather than a fantasy sheet, which for some players is the wrong room to be in.
